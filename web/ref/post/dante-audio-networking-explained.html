Dante audio networking, explained

Dante sends multichannel audio over standard networks. Here's why it simplifies event and install audio.

Short answer: Dante carries many channels of digital audio over a standard Ethernet network. One network cable can replace a heavy analog snake, and routing is changed in software.

How it works

Each Dante device, whether a mixer, stage box, amplifier or wireless receiver, appears on the network. A controller app shows every output and input as a grid, and you click to connect them.

Why crews like it

  • One Cat6 run instead of dozens of copper pairs
  • Latency low enough for live sound
  • Add a recording feed or overflow room without new cable
  • Redundant primary and secondary networks for critical shows

Where it fits

Corporate events, multi-room venues and permanent installs, anywhere sources and speakers are spread across a space. For a single small room, analog is still fine.

What the network needs

  • Managed gigabit switches with energy-saving modes turned off
  • A dedicated VLAN for audio
  • Quality of service set for clock and audio packets
  • No WiFi in the audio path

Common mistakes

  • Running Dante on the guest network
  • Mixed sample rates between devices
  • No secondary network on a show that cannot stop

The bottom line

Dante is reliable when the network is designed for it. Treat the switch like part of the sound system, because it is.
